Dispatch 4.2 — quiet responders, in brief
The short version, before you get to the console screen itself.
The issue

4.2 (shipped 12 Aug) broke two independent things at once. A callout offer timeout cut from 90 to 60 seconds costs every responder a live offer they'd already won. Separately, a scoring rule lets a responder's acceptance score sink to the floor with no way back — the only way to earn score is accepting an offer, and once ranked low enough, a responder stops being offered one at all.

Acceptance rate cratered 78.5% → 54.2% the week it shipped and has only partly recovered since; callout tickets ran at roughly triple normal volume, without tapering, from release through early September.

What we're proposing, and why

Two different fixes for two different failures — not one "revert":

  • Timeout — put the offer window back to a length that matches how long it actually takes to notice a phone and answer it.
  • Starvation — reset the four responders already stuck at the floor now; let a quiet stretch fade back toward neutral once a responder has stopped receiving offers entirely; back that fade with a guarantee that nobody goes indefinitely without at least one turn.

The fade is the exact question Wen left open next to the routing code in 2019 — she held off because a stretch where someone's still being reached and still declining shouldn't quietly get erased. Tying the fade to "stopped receiving offers entirely," not just time passing, is what makes it safe to finally build. This isn't the one-line fix engineering could ship this afternoon — that would clear today's four names without closing the hole that put them there.

Decisions needed from Helen
  1. Sign off on the direction — not the specific numbers, which still need Marcus and Wen.
  2. If it's a go: Marcus scopes the engineering work, Wen owns the routing design itself — it's her code, and this answers the question she left open in it.
  3. Timing — config ships inside a release with no runtime flag, so this needs either an out-of-cycle point release or the mid-September monthly train.
