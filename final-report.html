The short version

4.2 shipped three changes at once. Two of them broke the callout, each in its own way.

Failure 1: timeout, hits everyone

Offer window cut from 90s to 60s

Sixty seconds is shorter than it takes to reach the phone, read the offer and answer. Top-ranked responders lose offers they already won. Captain Vantage, a routing "winner", filed the first ticket.

Failure 2: starvation, hits a few

Proximity 0.45→0.60 on top of a score that can't recover

The acceptance score rises only when a responder accepts an offer. Once someone ranks low enough, they stop getting offers, so they can never accept one. The 2019 TODO in history.py flagged this, and 4.2 pushed four people into it.

Failure 3: silence

Neither change was acknowledged

Handlers saw the timeout cut in release notes on release morning. Nobody outside engineering heard about the scoring change.

Pooled acceptance rate, all 16 responders, weekly
share of offers accepted4.2 ships (12 Aug)

The rebound to 72.7% is partly a mirage. The starved responders dropped out of the offer pool, so the average went up while they stayed stuck.

The fix, as Kip would see it

Put the window back, show handlers what is happening, and give stuck responders a way back.

1

Revert the timeout: 60s → 90s

  • Timeout only, routing weights untouched
  • Ships as an out-of-cycle point release
  • Owner Marcus · approved by Marcus and Helen
2

Improve the dashboard for handlers

  • A score and trend for each responder, not just "gone quiet"
  • Daily detail: accepted, declined, timed out
  • An early warning days before offers stop
3

Recovery mode: one prioritized call every 3 days

  • A responder whose score drops below neutral enters recovery mode
  • They get 1 prioritized call every 3 days, ahead of their rank, to earn score back
  • Handlers see recovery mode on their dashboard, with no request needed

Module 4 X-Ray Vision

Starved responders weren't missing more calls. They stopped being asked at all.

My prompt · 04-x-ray-vision #12
why change in the closeness made such a bit impact on the ping? since according to the analytics we see that responders were equally missing the calls
Finding

offer.py's dispatch() stops at the first accept. Everyone ranked below that point is never offered the callout, which doesn't count as a decline or a timeout. That's why a starved responder's miss rate looks ordinary while their offer count drops to near zero. The penalty math didn't change in 4.2. The weights did.

The score's one-way door (unchanged since before 4.2)
start 0.505 misses → floor~7 accepts back

−0.12 per decline or timeout and +0.08 per accept. Five misses in a row take a responder from neutral to the floor. Getting back needs about seven accepts, and each one needs an offer that has to reach them first.

Ranking is illustrative. The stop-at-first-accept behavior is exactly what the code does.

Keeping it from happening again

A skill I wrote once, with four checks that each match a gap 4.2 shipped with.

/review-checklist grades any brief on four checks, in a fixed order. Each one gets Pass, Partial or Missing, with a quote as evidence and a one-line fix. It runs on demand and every Monday without me.

1

Problem before fix

Explain what's happening, to whom, and the evidence, before proposing a solution.

4.2: the 60s cut's roadmap driver was "Internal". No research on how responders actually answer came first.
2

User personas

Name the specific users affected and how, not just "users".

4.2: far-away and low-score responders weren't considered. Marcus still had to ask who the reweight would hit.
3

Agreed implementation plan

One chosen path, with who signed off or who still needs to.

4.2: nobody could say whether hitting decliners was a decision or a side effect.
4

Rollout / notification plan

Release vehicle, timing, and how affected users find out.

4.2: no rollout owner and nothing sent to responders. Handlers got release-morning notes.
Runs on demand and weekly

Before any brief goes out, and every Monday around 9:11 AM as a scheduled task.
